Use the friction model, including the maximum static friction condition, to analyze sliding and non-sliding situations.

Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

A brick is pulled at constant speed across a floor, first lying on its large face and then standing on its small end. How do the kinetic friction forces compare?

  1. Larger on the small end, because the pressure is greater.
  2. It cannot be compared without measuring both areas.
  3. Larger on the large face, because more surface touches the floor.
  4. They are equal: contact area does not affect friction.
Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

A box is dropped onto a conveyor belt that moves to the right. At first the box slides on the belt. Which way does kinetic friction from the belt act on the box while it slides?

  1. There is no friction until the box moves at the belt's speed.
  2. Straight down, pressing the box onto the belt.
  3. To the left, opposite to the belt's motion.
  4. To the right, opposing the box's sliding relative to the belt.
