Classical Mechanics: course outline

Practice for AP Physics C: Mechanics, organized by the official College Board units and topics. Each lesson lists its skills with sample problems. The problems are original practice problems written for this site, not College Board materials.

Open the practice app

Unit 1: Kinematics

  • 1.1 Scalars and Vectors
    Vector components, magnitude and direction, vector addition, and dot and cross products of vectors.
  • 1.2 Displacement, Velocity, and Acceleration
    Position, displacement, average and instantaneous velocity and acceleration, derivatives and integrals of motion, and constant acceleration.
  • 1.3 Representing Motion
    Reading and connecting position, velocity and acceleration graphs: slopes and areas.
  • 1.4 Reference Frames and Relative Motion
    Velocities measured in different reference frames.
  • 1.5 Motion in Two or Three Dimensions
    Position, velocity and acceleration vectors in 2D, and projectile motion.

Unit 2: Force and Translational Dynamics

  • 2.1 Systems and Center of Mass
    Locating the center of mass and how it moves.
  • 2.2 Forces and Free-Body Diagrams
    Identifying forces and drawing free-body diagrams; the normal force.
  • 2.3–2.4 Newton's Third Law and Newton's First Law
    Interaction pairs, inertia and equilibrium.
  • 2.5 Newton's Second Law
    Net force and acceleration, including tension, pulleys and connected objects.
  • 2.6 Gravitational Force
    Universal gravitation, weight and apparent weight.
  • 2.7 Kinetic and Static Friction
    Static and kinetic friction forces.
  • 2.8 Spring Forces
    Hooke's law and spring forces.
  • 2.9 Resistive Forces
    Velocity-dependent drag and terminal velocity.
  • 2.10 Circular Motion
    Centripetal acceleration and the net force in circular motion.

Unit 3: Work, Energy, and Power

  • 3.1–3.2 Translational Kinetic Energy and Work
    Kinetic energy, work by constant and variable forces, and the work–energy theorem.
  • 3.3 Potential Energy
    Conservative forces, potential energy functions and energy diagrams.
  • 3.4 Conservation of Energy
    Energy conservation and energy bar charts.
  • 3.5 Power
    Rate of energy transfer.

Unit 4: Linear Momentum

  • 4.1–4.2 Linear Momentum, Change in Momentum and Impulse
    Momentum and the impulse that changes it.
  • 4.3 Conservation of Linear Momentum
    Momentum conservation in isolated systems, including recoil.
  • 4.4 Elastic and Inelastic Collisions
    Classifying and solving collisions.

Unit 5: Torque and Rotational Dynamics

  • 5.1 Rotational Kinematics
    Angular position, velocity and acceleration.
  • 5.2 Connecting Linear and Rotational Motion
    v = rω, tangential and radial acceleration.
  • 5.3 Torque
    Torque from a force and lever arm; rotational equilibrium (5.5).
  • 5.4 Rotational Inertia
    Moment of inertia of objects and the parallel-axis theorem.
  • 5.6 Newton's Second Law in Rotational Form
    Net torque and angular acceleration.

Unit 6: Energy and Momentum of Rotating Systems

  • 6.3 Angular Momentum and Angular Impulse
    Angular momentum of particles and the angular impulse that changes it.
  • 6.4 Conservation of Angular Momentum
    Angular momentum of rigid bodies and its conservation.
  • 6.5 Rolling
    Rolling without slipping: kinematics, dynamics, and rotational kinetic energy (6.1–6.2).
  • 6.6 Motion of Orbiting Satellites
    Circular orbits: speed, period and energy.

Unit 7: Oscillations

  • 7.1–7.4 Simple Harmonic Motion
    Defining SHM, its period and frequency, representing it with graphs and equations, and its energy.
  • 7.5 Simple and Physical Pendulums
    Period of simple and physical pendulums.
