Classical Mechanics: course outline

Practice for AP Physics C: Mechanics in the Flipping Physics sequence, with each lesson checked against the College Board AP topics. Each lesson lists its skills with sample problems. The problems are original practice problems written for this site, not College Board materials.

Open the practice app

Unit 1: Kinematics

  • Lesson 1.1: Displacement, Velocity and Speed
    Position and displacement, average velocity and speed, and why velocity and speed differ.
  • Lesson 1.2: Acceleration and Motion Graphs
    Acceleration, and graphs of position, velocity and acceleration as functions of time.
  • Lesson 1.3: Uniformly Accelerated Motion
    The constant-acceleration (UAM) equations and how to choose and use them.
  • Lesson 1.4: Derivatives and Integrals in Kinematics
    Instantaneous velocity and acceleration as derivatives, and integrating to get velocity and position.
  • Lesson 1.5: Free Fall
    Motion under gravity alone: dropping, throwing up, and launching from a height.
  • Lesson 1.6: Vectors
    Vectors and scalars, vector components, and tip-to-tail vector addition.
  • Lesson 1.7: Projectile Motion
    Position, velocity and acceleration vectors in two dimensions, and projectile motion including the range equation.
  • Lesson 1.8: Relative Motion
    Frames of reference and relative velocity in one and two dimensions.

Unit 2: Dynamics: Newton's Laws and Forces

  • Lesson 2.1: Forces and Free-Body Diagrams
    Forces as interactions, identifying the forces on an object and drawing its free-body diagram, and weight versus mass.
  • Lesson 2.2: Newton's Laws of Motion
    Inertia and Newton's first law, Newton's second law, and Newton's third-law force pairs.
  • Lesson 2.3: Normal Force and Apparent Weight
    Finding the normal force with Newton's laws, on level ground, on inclines and with angled pushes, and the apparent weight of an accelerating person.
  • Lesson 2.4: Tension, Pulleys and Connected Objects
    The force of tension, connected objects, pulley systems, and ropes with mass.
  • Lesson 2.5: Friction and Inclines
    Static and kinetic friction, the coefficient of friction, and objects on inclines.
  • Lesson 2.6: Drag Force and Terminal Velocity
    Velocity-dependent resistive forces, terminal velocity, and solving for motion with drag.

Unit 3: Work, Energy, Power and Spring Force

  • Lesson 3.1: Spring Force and Hooke's Law
    The spring force, Hooke's law, springs in series and parallel, and measuring a spring constant.
  • Lesson 3.2: Work
    Work by a constant force, work as a dot product, and work by a variable force as an integral.
  • Lesson 3.3: Kinetic Energy and the Work-Energy Theorem
    Kinetic energy and the theorem that net work equals the change in kinetic energy.
  • Lesson 3.4: Potential Energy and Conservation of Mechanical Energy
    Gravitational and elastic potential energy, and conservation of mechanical energy.
  • Lesson 3.5: Conservative Forces, Energy Systems and Equilibrium
    Conservative and nonconservative forces, energy transferred into or out of a system, and potential energy graphs and equilibrium.
  • Lesson 3.6: Power
    Average and instantaneous power, and power with derivatives and integrals.

Unit 4: Momentum, Impulse and Center of Mass

  • Lesson 4.1: Momentum and Impulse
    Linear momentum, impact force, impulse, and impulse as the area under a force-time graph.
  • Lesson 4.2: Conservation of Momentum
    Conservation of linear momentum in explosions and interactions, in one and two dimensions.
  • Lesson 4.3: Elastic and Inelastic Collisions
    Elastic, inelastic and perfectly inelastic collisions, and the ballistic pendulum.
  • Lesson 4.4: Center of Mass
    The center of mass of particles and of rigid objects, and how a system's center of mass moves.

Unit 5: Rotational Kinematics

  • Lesson 5.1: Angular Position, Velocity and Acceleration
    Arc length, angular velocity, angular acceleration and uniformly angularly accelerated motion.
  • Lesson 5.2: Tangential Velocity and Acceleration
    Connecting angular and linear motion: the speed and tangential acceleration of a point on a rotating body.
  • Lesson 5.3: Centripetal Acceleration and Circular Motion
    Centripetal acceleration, total acceleration when the speed changes, and applying Newton's second law to circular motion.

Unit 6: Rotational Dynamics

  • Lesson 6.1: Moment of Inertia
    Moment of inertia of particles and rigid objects, derivations by integration, and the parallel axis theorem.
  • Lesson 6.2: Torque and Rotational Equilibrium
    Torque, torque as a cross product, and rotational and static equilibrium.
  • Lesson 6.3: Rotational Form of Newton's Second Law
    Net torque equals rotational inertia times angular acceleration, including pulleys with mass.
  • Lesson 6.4: Rotational Work and Kinetic Energy
    Rotational kinetic energy, work done by a torque, and the rotational work-energy theorem.
  • Lesson 6.5: Rolling Without Slipping
    Rolling kinematics, rolling down an incline, and rolling kinetic energy.
  • Lesson 6.6: Angular Momentum
    Angular momentum of particles and rigid objects, and how torque changes angular momentum.
  • Lesson 6.7: Conservation of Angular Momentum
    Conservation of angular momentum for spinning objects and rotational collisions.

Unit 7: Universal Gravitation and Kepler's Laws

  • Lesson 7.1: Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation
    The gravitational force between masses, the gravitational field, and g on other planets.
  • Lesson 7.2: Circular Orbits
    Satellites in circular orbits, geostationary orbit, and apparent weightlessness.
  • Lesson 7.3: Kepler's Third Law and Elliptical Orbits
    Kepler's third law, and conservation of angular momentum and energy along an elliptical orbit.
  • Lesson 7.4: Gravitational Potential Energy and Escape Velocity
    Universal gravitational potential energy, the energy of a satellite, binding energy and escape velocity.

Unit 8: Simple Harmonic Motion

  • Lesson 8.1: Simple Harmonic Motion and Its Period
    The restoring force of SHM, and the period and frequency of a mass-spring system.
  • Lesson 8.2: SHM Equations and Graphs
    Position, velocity and acceleration equations and graphs, and the phase constant.
  • Lesson 8.3: Energy in Simple Harmonic Motion
    Total mechanical energy, and how kinetic and potential energy trade off during an oscillation.
  • Lesson 8.4: Simple and Physical Pendulums
    The simple pendulum and the physical pendulum as simple harmonic oscillators.
