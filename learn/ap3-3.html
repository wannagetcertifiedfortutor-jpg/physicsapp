Conservative vs non-conservative forces

Test path independence; recognize which forces admit a potential energy.

Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

An object is moved along a closed path that starts and ends at the same point. For which of the following forces is the total work done on the object around the path necessarily zero?

  1. The air resistance force
  2. The force exerted by a person pushing the object along the path
  3. The kinetic friction force exerted by the floor
  4. The gravitational force exerted by earth
Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

A particle is carried counter-clockwise around the square with corners (0,0), (1,0), (1,1), (0,1) (meters) under the force F=2(y^+x^​) (newtons, with x,y in meters). Evaluate the work W=∮F⋅dr around the loop and interpret it.

  1. W=0, which proves the force is conservative because the loop integral vanished for this one path.
  2. W=2 J; the top and right sides contribute but the bottom and left do not.
  3. W=2⋅2⋅12=4 J; the force is not conservative.
  4. W=0 around this loop, consistent with the force being conservative (indeed F=∇(αxy)).

Potential energy functions

Derive U(x) from a conservative force by integration and choose reference points.

Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

Two students analyze the same ball thrown upward. One sets Ug​=0 at the ground, the other at the launch height. Which quantities will they disagree on?

  1. The maximum height reached, since it depends on how much potential energy is available.
  2. Nothing — the potential energy itself is the same for both students.
  3. The speed at the top, because the student with the lower zero level assigns more potential energy there.
  4. Only the value of Ug​ at any given instant; ΔUg​, ΔK and the speed at every point agree.
Sample problem · AP Free Response · Calculate

A 2 kg book sits on a shelf 2 m above the floor. A table top is 1.5 m above the floor. Taking the gravitational potential energy to be zero at the table top (which is 1.5 m above the floor), what is the book's potential energy U? Use g=9.8 m/s².

Potential energy diagrams

Use F = -dU/dx to find equilibria, their stability, and turning points.

Sample problem · AP Free Response · Analyze the Graph

The graph shows U(x) for a particle confined to the right-hand well. Its total mechanical energy is E=1 J (shown as the horizontal line). What is the largest x it reaches (the right-hand turning point)? (The curve is U=41​(x2−4)2 J.)

Sample problem · AP Free Response · Derive

A block on a spring also feels a constant force F in the +x direction (for example, gravity for a vertical spring). Its potential energy is U(x)=21​kx2−Fx, with x measured from the spring's natural length. Derive the equilibrium position x0​, in terms of k and F.
