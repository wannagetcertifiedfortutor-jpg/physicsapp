Instantaneous velocity as a derivative

Differentiate position functions to obtain v(t); read velocity as the slope of x(t).

Sample problem · AP Free Response · Calculate

The position of a bead is x(t)=0.5t3−6t (SI units). What is its velocity at t=1.5 s?

Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

A particle has position x(t)=2(t−3)2−3 (SI units). When is its velocity zero?

  1. Never — the particle is always moving because x changes with t.
  2. At the time when x=0.
  3. At t=3 s, where the slope of x(t) is zero.
  4. At t=0, because the particle starts from rest.

Acceleration as a derivative

Differentiate v(t) or x(t) twice; interpret the sign of acceleration relative to velocity.

Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

A car moves in the −x direction and is slowing down. Which pair of signs is correct?

  1. vx​>0 and ax​>0
  2. vx​<0 and ax​>0
  3. vx​>0 and ax​<0
  4. vx​<0 and ax​<0
Sample problem · AP Free Response · Calculate

A drone's altitude is y(t)=0.5t3−3t2 (SI units, +y up). Find its acceleration at t=2 s.

Integrating acceleration to velocity and position

Use definite integrals and initial conditions to obtain v(t) and x(t) from a(t), including non-constant acceleration.

Sample problem · AP Free Response · Calculate

A sled on ice has acceleration ax​(t)=0.5t (SI units) and velocity vx​(0)=4 m/s. Find vx​ at t=2 s.

Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

A cart moves along a straight track. Its acceleration is ax​(t)=2t+4, with ax​ in m/s² and t in seconds. At t=0 its velocity is 2 m/s. What is its velocity at t=2 s?

  1. 12 m/s
  2. 18 m/s
  3. 8 m/s
  4. 14 m/s
