Conservative vs non-conservative forces

Test path independence; recognize which forces admit a potential energy.

Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

A 6 kg box is slid 15 m across a level floor and then slid back to where it started. The coefficient of kinetic friction is 0.3. What is the total work done on the box by friction during the round trip?

  1. −265 J
  2. 529 J
  3. 0 J
  4. −529 J
Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

A 5 kg sled slides from the top of a hill to the bottom along a winding path 20 m long. The bottom is 10 m lower than the top. How much work does gravity do on the sled?

  1. 245 J
  2. 980 J
  3. 490 J
  4. −490 J

Energy bar charts

Track energy transfers between kinetic, potential and thermal forms.

Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

The bar chart shows the energy of a system at one instant: K=15 J, Ug​=5 J, Us​=0, Eth​=0 (total 20 J). Which situation is consistent with this chart?

  1. A block sliding on a rough floor, slowing down.
  2. A block compressed against a spring and at rest.
  3. A ball at the very top of its flight.
  4. A ball thrown upward, part-way to its highest point, on a frictionless (no-drag) flight.
Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

A block on a frictionless horizontal surface is pushed against a spring, compressing it, and held at rest. The bar chart shows the energy of the block–spring system: 16 J of spring potential energy. The block is released. Which of the following describes the system's energy at the moment the block leaves the spring?

  1. Kinetic energy 16 J, spring energy 16 J
  2. Kinetic energy 8 J, spring energy 0 J
  3. Kinetic energy 8 J, spring energy 8 J
  4. Kinetic energy 16 J, spring energy 0 J

Potential energy diagrams

Use F = -dU/dx to find equilibria, their stability, and turning points.

Sample problem · AP Free Response · Analyze the Graph

The graph shows U(x) for a particle confined to the right-hand well. Its total mechanical energy is E=1 J (shown as the horizontal line). What is the largest x it reaches (the right-hand turning point)? (The curve is U=41​(x2−4)2 J.)

Sample problem · AP Free Response · Derive

A block on a spring also feels a constant force F in the +x direction (for example, gravity for a vertical spring). Its potential energy is U(x)=21​kx2−Fx, with x measured from the spring's natural length. Derive the equilibrium position x0​, in terms of k and F.
