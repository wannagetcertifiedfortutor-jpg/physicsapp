Kinetic energy, work as an integral of force, the work–kinetic-energy theorem in 1D, power.

Work in 1D

Compute work by integrating a position-dependent force; interpret area under F(x).

Sample problem · AP Free Response · Analyze the Graph

The graph shows the x-component of the net force on a 2 kg cart as it moves along the +x axis. How much work does this force do on the cart between x=0 and x=6 m?

Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

You lower a heavy box slowly at constant speed from a shelf to the floor, holding it the whole way. During the descent, which statement about the work done is correct?

  1. Your hands do positive work because you are exerting an upward force.
  2. Neither force does work because the speed does not change.
  3. Gravity does negative work because the box ends up lower.
  4. Gravity does positive work and your hands do negative work; the two cancel.

Work–kinetic-energy theorem

Derive and apply W_total = ΔK.

Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

A car skids to a stop on a level road with its wheels locked. If the car's initial speed were 2 times greater (same road, same car), how would the skidding distance compare?

  1. 2 times as far, because the friction force is also 2 times larger at higher speed.
  2. The same distance, because friction removes a fixed amount of energy per unit mass.
  3. 2 times as far, because stopping distance is proportional to speed.
  4. 4 times as far, because the kinetic energy to be removed is 4 times larger while the friction force is unchanged.
Sample problem · AP Free Response · Calculate

A 2 kg hockey puck slides across rough ice at 8 m/s. The coefficient of kinetic friction is μk​=0.15. Using the work–kinetic-energy theorem, find how far the puck slides before stopping. Take g=9.8 m/s².

Power

Compute instantaneous and average power as dW/dt and F·v.

Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

A ball is thrown at an angle and follows a parabolic arc (no drag). At the highest point of its flight, the instantaneous power delivered by gravity to the ball is …

  1. −mgv, where v is the ball's speed there, because gravity opposes the motion.
  2. mgv, where v is the ball's speed there.
  3. zero, because the ball is momentarily at rest.
  4. zero, because the velocity is horizontal and perpendicular to the gravitational force.
Sample problem · AP Free Response · Calculate

A construction hoist raises a 200 kg load straight up at a steady 0.4 m/s. What power does the hoist's cable deliver to the load? Take g=9.8 m/s².
