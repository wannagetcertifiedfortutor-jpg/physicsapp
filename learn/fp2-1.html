Identifying forces and free-body diagrams

Identify every force exerted on an object, each from an interaction with another object, and draw it on a free-body diagram with the correct direction.

Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

A block is pulled at constant speed across a rough horizontal floor by a horizontal rope. Which of the following lists all the forces exerted on the block?

  1. Gravitational force, tension, and kinetic friction
  2. Gravitational force, normal force, tension, and kinetic friction
  3. Gravitational force, normal force, and tension
  4. Gravitational force, normal force, tension, kinetic friction, and a forward force of motion
Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

A box sits at rest on a rough ramp without sliding. Which list contains exactly the forces exerted on it, as they should appear on its free-body diagram?

  1. Gravity, the normal force, and static friction pointing down the ramp
  2. Gravity, the normal force, static friction, and a separate 'downhill force'
  3. Gravity and the normal force only, because the box is not moving
  4. Gravity, the normal force, and static friction pointing up the ramp

Weight and mass

Distinguish mass (inertia, in kg) from weight (the gravitational force, W = mg) on Earth and other worlds, and inertial versus gravitational mass.

Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

On Mars, where g=3.71 N/kg, a spring scale shows that a sample has a weight of 730 N. What is the sample's mass?

  1. 197 kg
  2. 74.5 kg
  3. 2710 kg
  4. 730 kg
Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

An astronaut has mass 60 kg on Earth. On the Moon, where the free-fall acceleration is 1.6 m/s², what are the astronaut's mass and weight?

  1. Mass 9.8 kg, weight 588 N.
  2. Mass 9.8 kg, weight 96 N.
  3. Mass 60 kg, weight 96 N.
  4. Mass 60 kg, weight 588 N.
