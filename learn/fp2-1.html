Free-body diagrams

Draw correct free-body diagrams: every force present, none extra, correct directions.

Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

A block is pulled at constant speed across a rough horizontal floor by a horizontal rope. Which of the following lists all the forces exerted on the block?

  1. Gravitational force, tension, and kinetic friction
  2. Gravitational force, normal force, tension, and kinetic friction
  3. Gravitational force, normal force, and tension
  4. Gravitational force, normal force, tension, kinetic friction, and a forward force of motion
Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

A block of mass 18.5 kg slides down a frictionless ramp inclined at 20∘. From its free-body diagram, what is the block's acceleration?

  1. 9.8 m/s²
  2. 3.57 m/s²
  3. 9.21 m/s²
  4. 3.35 m/s²

Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

  1. Mass 9.8 kg, weight 588 N.
  2. Mass 9.8 kg, weight 96 N.
  3. Mass 60 kg, weight 96 N.
  4. Mass 60 kg, weight 588 N.
Sample problem · AP Free Response · Calculate

A 65 kg student stands on a bathroom scale in an elevator that is moving upward but slowing down with acceleration of magnitude 1.2 m/s². What force does the scale read (in newtons)? Take g=9.8 m/s².

Normal force

Determine the normal force on inclines and in accelerating situations.

Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

Which statement best describes the normal force between two surfaces in contact?

  1. It is always vertical, because it supports the object against gravity.
  2. It adjusts to whatever value is needed to prevent the objects from passing through each other (up to the surface's breaking point).
  3. It is the reaction force to gravity, required by Newton's third law.
  4. It always equals the object's weight mg.
Sample problem · AP Free Response · Calculate

A 3 kg block rests on a frictionless incline of angle 45∘ (held momentarily at rest by a small side stop that exerts no force perpendicular to the surface). What is the normal force the incline exerts on the block? Take g=9.8 m/s².
