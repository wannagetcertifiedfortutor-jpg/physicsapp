Universal gravitation

Apply F = G m1 m2 / r² and derive g from G, M and R.

Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

The distance between two point masses is tripled. The gravitational force between them becomes …

  1. 1/6 of its original value
  2. 3 times its original value
  3. 1/3 of its original value
  4. 1/9 of its original value
Sample problem · AP Free Response · Calculate

A 60 kg person stands 3 m (center to center) from a 1000 kg boulder. Treating both as point masses and using G=6.674×10−11 N·m²/kg², what is the magnitude of the gravitational force between them?

Weight, mass and apparent weight

Distinguish weight from mass; compute apparent weight in accelerating frames.

Sample problem · AP Multiple Choice

An astronaut has mass 60 kg on Earth. On the Moon, where the free-fall acceleration is 1.6 m/s², what are the astronaut's mass and weight?

  1. Mass 9.8 kg, weight 588 N.
  2. Mass 9.8 kg, weight 96 N.
  3. Mass 60 kg, weight 96 N.
  4. Mass 60 kg, weight 588 N.
Sample problem · AP Free Response · Calculate

A 65 kg student stands on a bathroom scale in an elevator that is moving upward but slowing down with acceleration of magnitude 1.2 m/s². What force does the scale read (in newtons)? Take g=9.8 m/s².
